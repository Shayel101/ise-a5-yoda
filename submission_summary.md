Vioque et al. (2020) argue that their YODA neural network tolerates spurious WISE W3/W4 photometry and catalogue cross-match errors. I tested two claims frozen in advance. C1: at paper-realistic fault rates, classes and PMS recall stay stable. C2: malformed input fails loudly. No weights or labels were published, so I re-implemented the pipeline. It has 187 SIMBAD-labelled stars and 3 bootstrap models (quick mode). It recovers 64% of the published PMS candidates and 95% of the CBe candidates. Before any injection I froze the environment (uv.lock), the data checksums, the seeds, a 4.15M-row catalogue, the thresholds and the stopping rule.

C1 was falsified. When W3/W4 values are resampled at the paper's own 44% rate, 10.5% [95% CI 10.0–11.0] of PMS candidates and 9.5% of CBe candidates change class, and PMS recall drops 8.9 pp. Cross-match errors at 2.3% and 1.3% caused under 2% flips, which are negative results. C2 was falsified: impossible magnitudes, swapped columns and duplicate IDs received normal probabilities. YODA's float handling silently corrupted 636 of 1,000 Gaia IDs, and its averaging script corrupted 791 of 1,000.

The single improvement was an input-validation and identifier-integrity guard. Under the identical protocol it passed every C2 case and preserved all IDs. It wrongly rejected 1.6–2.3% of the published candidates. It did not fix C1: it turned some silent misclassifications into rejections, so the frozen flip rates rose. The averaging script still corrupts IDs. Residual risks and the rollback plan are documented in the repository.
